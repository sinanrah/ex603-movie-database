# Unit 4 Finding: The Actual Spam Rate and Where It Comes From

**To:** the leadership team
**From:** Sinan Rahman, Trust and Data team

The old dashboard reported that about half of all ratings were withdrawn as spam. That number came from a filter that left out every rating with no withdrawal reason. Those ratings were never withdrawn, so they belong in the total as normal ratings. Counted correctly, 12 of the 200 ratings on the platform were withdrawn as spam, a spam rate of 6%. By region, it ranges from about 2% in Vietnam and the UK to 10% in India.

The rate is low for most titles. 13 of the 15 titles in the catalogue have ratings. Four of them have no spam at all, and most of the rest have only one spam rating. Looking only at titles with more ratings than average, which gives the most reliable picture, the combined spam rate is 4.8%. That is a fair baseline for what a normal title looks like.

Two titles stand out, and I would investigate them:

- **Title ID 2:** 3 of its 16 ratings were spam (18.8%), almost four times the baseline. The three spam ratings came from three different users.
- **Title ID 3:** 2 of its 13 ratings were spam (15.4%), about three times the baseline. This title is also currently unavailable in the catalogue.

Title ID 4 also has a high rate (1 of 6, or 16.7%), but with only 6 ratings, a single spam rating is enough to make it look high.

**Recommendation:** open a spam review for title IDs 2 and 3, starting with the users behind their spam ratings, to see whether the problem is the titles themselves or a few accounts. Keep watching title ID 4 as it gets more ratings. No platform-wide action is needed, since the overall rate is 6% and most titles are within a few points of the baseline.
