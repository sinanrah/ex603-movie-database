# Unit 6 Finding: Is the Spam Problem Getting Better or Worse?

To: the leadership team  
From: Sinan Rahman, Trust and Data team

The spam problem got slightly better over 2023, but it was small to begin with and the improvement is not strong. By quarter, the share of ratings withdrawn as spam went from 7.5% in Q1 (4 of 53 ratings) to 6.3% in Q2 (3 of 48), 5.9% in Q3 (3 of 51), and 4.2% in Q4 (2 of 48). The first half of the year had 7 spam ratings out of 101 (6.9%) and the second half had 5 out of 99 (5.1%). These come from adding up the monthly counts in query 6.3B and the quarter totals in query 6.2B.

The month-by-month numbers look much worse than that, and they should not be used to make decisions on their own. In query 6.3B the spam rate goes from 18.2% in March to 0% in April, but March was only 2 spam ratings out of 11, and four months had no spam at all. With about one spam rating a month, a single account can make one month look like a spike. December's 12.5% is the same kind of month, 2 spam ratings out of 16.

The one title where spam kept coming back is Title_02. Query 6.2A shows spam ratings on it in January, March, and December. Title_02 was already one of the two titles flagged in Units 4 and 5.

Rating activity itself is steady. Every quarter ended between 48 and 53 ratings (query 6.2B). The longest stretch without ratings was Title_09, with 113 days between January 5 and April 28 (query 6.2C). Other titles kept getting rated during that time, so the platform did not stop recording ratings. Title_09 is marked unavailable today, but the data does not show when that changed.

What I recommend for next quarter:

1. For the platform as a whole: do not freeze reviewer payouts or start a platform-wide spam crackdown. Spam is about 5% of ratings and went down over the year.
2. For the spam reporting: report the spam rate as an average over the last three months instead of one month at a time, so a quiet month with one or two spam ratings does not look like a spike.
3. For Title_02: keep the spam review going, including its December spam rating, since this title's spam kept coming back all year.
4. For Title_09: have the catalogue team check when it became unavailable, to see whether that is why it went 113 days without a rating.

What the data cannot tell us yet: there are only 200 ratings and 12 spam ratings in one year, so the drop from 7.5% to 4.2% could still be chance. Another two or three quarters of data would show whether it is a real improvement. The data also only stores whether a title is available today, not when that changed, so the Title_09 question needs the catalogue's own history to answer.
